## The problem

My employer, a company that rents out office space, keeps the status of every room in the CRM: available, under option or rented. That status was no longer correct.

The cause was automations that overwrote each other. One function set the tenant of a room again on every change to a deal. As a result, tenants seemed to move from one room to another.

## The approach

I wrote a pipeline in Python that compared the rooms in the CRM with two other sources. The first was the live floor plan data, which is the real source of truth. The second was the cancellation forms.

Names are rarely written the same way twice, so I used fuzzy matching with rapidfuzz. That is a way to match texts that are almost, but not exactly, the same.

Some things a script cannot guess, such as different spellings of the same company name or tenants who moved within a building. I wrote that business knowledge down as explicit rules.

After that I rewrote the two faulty Deluge functions in the CRM. They no longer overwrite a room that is rented.

## The result

About 1,135 rooms were sorted into four groups: correct automatically, confirmed by hand, needs checking and already correct. The team got this as a clear Excel workbook.

The source of the bad data is closed, so the problem does not come back.
